Why do tiny protocells stick together?

What am I seeing?

Attraction currently wins.

DLVO combines three effects: a sticky van der Waals attraction, a charged electrostatic coat that pushes other protocells away, and a very short-range core bumper. At 20 nm the coat is almost gone and the attraction wins, so the pair tends to move closer. Random thermal kicks still make the motion jagged.

Show the equations and model limits

Signed forces used by the demo

F_vdW = −A_H · R_eff / (6 L²) F_el = (B / λ_D) · exp(−L / λ_D) F_core = 12 C · L_core¹² / L¹³ r(L) = 1 / (1 + L / L_ret)² F_CL,ret = F_vdW · r(L) L_next = L + μ F Δt + √(2DΔt) · ξ

Negative force attracts; positive force repels. SI units are used internally, with forces shown in pN. R = 500 nm, R_eff = R/2, A_H = 0.5 or 5 × 10⁻²¹ J, B = 20 k_BT, C = 0.5 k_BT, L_core = 0.5 nm, L_ret = 20 nm, Δt = 10⁻⁷ s, μ = 10⁸ m/(N s), and pair forces are capped at ±20 pN.

What this page does not claim

  • CL replaces vdW; CL and vdW are compared, never added. They describe the same fluctuation-induced attraction at different levels of approximation.
  • Retardation here is a simplified educational attenuation factor, not a full Lifshitz or Matsubara calculation. It only weakens near-field attraction.
  • In the full 2D model, vdW and CL have no core repulsion and can produce deep overlap. This pair view clamps the gap to 0.5–200 nm instead; that clamp is not a core force.
  • This pair-dynamics demonstration is not evidence of spontaneous cluster formation. The exported many-particle simulations start from preclustered lattices.
  • More salt shortens DLVO’s electrostatic reach; it does not switch off vdW or CL attraction. A stronger thermal setting means larger random kicks, not a guaranteed outcome.
